Classic DirectDraw software surfaces
==================================

Each group below comes from the native wxddver.exe process and its application-local early/ddraw.dll. The test creates the selected device and surface interfaces, fills white and red rows, copies them, writes one arbitrary RGB value through a subrectangle lock, verifies all 12 source pixels and samples three window pixels after a stretched primary blit. DirectDraw 2 creates a version 1 surface and also exercises its version 2 and version 3 interfaces. DirectDraw 4 and 7 use their distinct surface descriptions and rectangle-based Unlock calls. The version 7 device is constructed through DirectDrawCreateEx.

Groups mentioning GDI DC also draw one blue pixel with SetPixel, release the DC and verify the changed pixel and an untouched 24-bit color by locking the surface again. Groups mentioning the 8-bit palette exercise all four devices, change a palette entry, preserve destinations covered by the source color key and convert fifteen RGB pixels back into their palette indices.

Groups mentioning clip rectangles also validate a disjoint explicit region and a window-derived clip list. The metadata line covers a snapshot of live surfaces, version 4/7 private data expiring after a uniqueness change, and reverse lookup of a surface from its GDI DC.

Primary presentation uses a 32-bit windowed GDI software surface on the running desktop. Driver identification identifies the WINDX software adapter. Exclusive display modes, overlays and 3D rendering are outside these surface tests.
